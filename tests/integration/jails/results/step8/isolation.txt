## Result
Blocked as expected (TCP, no answer, dropped): hearst 10.79.20.170 on 22
and 8443; merrick 10.79.20.80:22; 10.79.20.1:443; samples in 172.16/12
and 192.168/16 (no answer at all; from the jail alone a drop cannot be
told from an absent host, so OPNsense's log is the real evidence there).

**Unexpected: ICMP echo passes into 10/8.** From the jail, ping gets
replies from hearst (10.79.20.170), merrick (10.79.20.80), 10.79.20.1,
and 10.79.10.250, all with ttl 63, i.e. routed through 10.79.60.1. The
capture on hearst's vtnet0 shows the echo request from 10.79.60.104
arriving and hearst replying. So the LAB rules let ICMP echo from VLAN
60 into the management network; TCP is dropped. This is the firewall's
rule set, not Skep; nothing was changed. 10.0.0.1 (no host) did not
reply.

Allowed, as expected: DNS to 10.79.10.250 and .249 (TCP 53 open; A
records through a CNAME; host(1) resolves). 10.79.60.1 does not answer
DNS. Internet: ping 9.9.9.9, TCP 1.1.1.1:443, fetch of
https://www.freebsd.org/ all work. NTP: 10.79.60.1 answers a hand-made
client query (48 bytes), and so does 162.159.200.1; both missed some
back-to-back tries, most likely the servers' rate limiting, so this
shows reachability, not reliability.

hearst on any address: hearst's only address outside lo0 is
10.79.20.170 (bridge0, vtnet1, and the epair host end have none; IPv6 is
disabled on them). TCP to it is dropped; ICMP echo reaches it (above).
127.0.0.1 and ::1 in a VNET jail are the jail's own loopback. With the
jail end's IPv6 enabled for the probe, ff02::1 on VLAN 60 was answered
only by the jail itself: hearst has no link-local presence on the jail's
link.
